# Cohort Analysis & Retention Modeling

## 1. Concept & Methodology
Cohort analysis segments customer populations by their acquisition date (the calendar month of their first order: `acquisition_month`) and observes their subsequent transactional activity across progressive monthly periods (`months_since_acquisition`: Period 0, Period 1, Period 2, ... Period 12+).

---

## 2. Retention Matrix Grain & Formulation
- **Table**: `analytics.customer_cohorts`
- **Grain**: Exactly one row per `(acquisition_month, activity_month)`.
- **Primary Metric**:
  $$\text{Retention Rate} = \frac{\text{Retained Customers in Period } n}{\text{Initial Cohort Size in Period 0}} \times 100\%$$
- **Attributes**:
  - `cohort_month`: YYYY-MM formatted string of first purchase.
  - `cohort_size`: Total count of unique customers acquired in `cohort_month`.
  - `period_number`: Integer index representing calendar months elapsed ($n = 0, 1, 2, ...$).
  - `active_customers`: Count of unique customers from this cohort who placed at least one order in period $n$.
  - `total_period_revenue`: Sum of merchandise spend generated by this cohort in period $n$.
  - `retention_rate`: Percentage of cohort active in period $n$.

---

## 3. Empirical Observations in Olist E-commerce
- Month 0 (Acquisition Month): Retention is naturally 100% by definition.
- Month 1: Retention drops sharply to ~0.4% - 0.7%, typical for broad non-grocery multi-vendor consumer goods platforms where major purchases (appliances, furniture, bedding) occur on multi-year lifecycles.
- Long-tail Retention: Subsequent periods maintain a steady 0.3% - 0.5% return rate.
- Business Implication: Growth is predominantly acquisition-driven, but customer retention programs focusing on high-frequency consumable categories (beauty, health, pet supplies) represent the highest return on marketing investment.
